## Page Content

## Find a guide by immigration question



Use the category tabs to browse by topic. Start with the question you need to answer—such as whether you may qualify for a work permit, which documents a sponsorship application needs or how an Express Entry round works—then open the guide that matches that issue.



- Express Entry scores, eligibility and invitation rounds
- Work permits, study permits and post-graduation options
- Family sponsorship, visitor visas and program updates

## Use an article to plan your research



An article is a starting point for research, not a review of your individual file. Note the program, dates and documents discussed, then compare them with your own status and application history. If a refusal, deadline, employer or family detail changes the facts, a general guide may not answer the question on its own.



- Read the article’s publication or update date
- Open the official links for the current rule
- Check whether your facts fit the described situation

## Check current program rules and dates



Immigration rules, intake windows, fees and forms can change. Before relying on an eligibility detail or planning a filing date, open the linked IRCC, provincial, territorial or tribunal page and confirm it still reflects the current instructions. For draw articles, treat past cutoffs as historical records rather than predictions.



- Confirm the rule and page update date
- Check the current application guide and fee
- Keep copies of notices and submitted documents

## Read official sources before you apply



For the federal pathway overview, start with the [IRCC immigration and citizenship programs page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada.html). For Express Entry, compare your research with the [official Express Entry guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry.html). Provincial and territorial streams have separate criteria and official updates.



- Follow the official source linked in the relevant guide
- Check the page date, forms and fees before filing
- Get individual advice when your history affects eligibility

## Official sources and responsible advice



For the current rule, review the [official IRCC immigration hub](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada.html) and compare it with the [verify an RCIC on the CICC register](https://register.college-ic.ca/Public-Register-EN/Public-Register-EN/Default.aspx). When the issue is personal, urgent or legally complex, ask an authorized representative or lawyer to review the complete record.



- verify current requirements
- keep a complete file copy
- ask who is authorized to help

## Questions people ask



### Where can I find Canadian immigration guides by program or application type?

Browse the category tabs for Express Entry and PNP guides, work and study permit information, family sponsorship, visitor visas and refusal topics. Open the guide closest to your question, then use its official source links to check the current rule.

### Are Canada immigration blog articles a substitute for IRCC instructions?

No. An article can explain a process in plain language, but the current IRCC or provincial instructions control. The decision-maker assesses the application using the rules and evidence that apply to the case.

### How do I check whether an immigration guide is still current?

Check the article’s date and open its linked government or tribunal pages. Verify the program status, eligibility, forms, fee and deadline again before submitting an application or making a travel or work decision.

## A practical next step



If an article raises a question about your own status, history or deadline, [book a consultation with a licensed RCIC](https://commonwealthmigration.ca/contact/book-immigration-consultation-canada). Bring the guide, your key dates and relevant documents so the discussion can focus on the rule and evidence that apply to your situation.
