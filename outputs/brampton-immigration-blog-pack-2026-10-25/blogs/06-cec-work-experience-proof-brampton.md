## Build a record that matches across documents

For every job you may claim, collect an employer letter with dates, title, duties, hours per week, pay, and employer contact details. Keep pay statements, tax slips, contracts, and schedules where available. Your NOC should match the duties you performed, not just the wording on a badge or pay stub.

CEC experience has limits. IRCC does not count some kinds of work, including work gained while studying full-time, co-op work terms, volunteer or unpaid work, and work performed without legal authorization. The rules also cap how many hours can be counted each week, so overtime should not be used to shorten the required period.

If there are gaps, leave, remote work, changes in status, or inconsistent employer records, write down the facts and identify what evidence can support them. Do not inflate hours or combine unrelated duties to fit a code.

## How to count and prove qualifying experience

Build a week-by-week timeline that shows when each job began and ended, the hours worked, and whether you were authorized to work. IRCC caps the hours that can count in a week, so working overtime does not let you reach 1,560 hours in fewer calendar months. If you worked part time, more calendar time may be needed to reach the required total.

For each employer, request a letter on company letterhead that includes your start and end dates, title, hours per week, wage, and a detailed list of main duties. The duties should be specific enough to compare with the NOC description. Add pay statements, tax slips, contracts, schedules, or other records that help confirm the same facts. If a former employer has closed or cannot provide a complete letter, preserve the contact attempts and identify reliable alternative evidence.

Check that the work was paid, performed in Canada, authorized, and in an eligible skilled occupation. Do not count work performed while a full-time student where IRCC excludes it, a required co-op placement, volunteer work, or unauthorized work. Self-employment is generally not counted for CEC. When a job changed substantially, separate the periods and duties rather than using a single NOC code for the entire time.

Eligibility is only one stage. You also need to meet the language threshold for your occupation’s TEER level and qualify for an Express Entry-managed program. Meeting CEC criteria does not guarantee an invitation; the CRS score and invitation-round rules still matter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do part-time jobs count toward CEC?

They may count if the work meets the CEC requirements. The hours can be accumulated across qualifying jobs, but IRCC limits the number of hours counted per week, so document each job and the dates carefully.

### Can I count overtime to reach 1,560 hours faster?

No. IRCC applies a weekly cap when calculating the required experience. Extra hours in one week do not let you shorten the required period beyond that rule.

### Does a Brampton job automatically qualify as Canadian experience?

No. The location is not enough. The job must be paid skilled work in Canada, performed with authorization, and meet the CEC occupation, duties, duration, and other criteria.
