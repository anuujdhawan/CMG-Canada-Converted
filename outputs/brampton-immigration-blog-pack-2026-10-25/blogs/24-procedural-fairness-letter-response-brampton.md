## Quick answer

A procedural fairness letter (PFL) gives an applicant a chance to respond to a concern before IRCC or another decision-maker makes a decision. The deadline and submission instructions in the letter control. Identify each concern, answer it with accurate facts and relevant evidence, and get urgent case-specific advice if the issue could involve misrepresentation or inadmissibility.

A procedural fairness letter (PFL) is a notice that raises a concern and gives the applicant an opportunity to respond before a decision is made. It is not the same as a refusal. Read the letter carefully: it identifies the concern, the information or documents at issue, and the deadline for responding. The deadline in your letter controls; there is no single response period that applies to every case.

## Organize the response before drafting

1. Record the deadline and the exact concern raised.
2. Gather the application, supporting documents, prior correspondence, and the specific record the officer is questioning.
3. Build a factual timeline and identify what evidence addresses each point.
4. Explain discrepancies directly and consistently; do not ignore unfavourable facts.
5. Check translation, file format, and submission instructions, then retain proof of delivery.

If the issue involves possible misrepresentation, criminality, medical inadmissibility, or a serious status consequence, get case-specific professional advice quickly. A response can have long-term consequences. Do not send a generic template or invent a supporting explanation.

IRCC now sends officer decision notes with most temporary-resident refusal letters and most permanent-resident refusal letters, but those notes are different from a PFL and do not replace a response opportunity. See the current [officer decision notes notice](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/officer-decision-notes.html).

For applicants in Brampton, keep the response focused on the facts in the file. A long submission is not automatically stronger; the decision-maker should be able to see the concern, answer, and supporting document without searching through unrelated material.


## Structure a clear response under the letter’s deadline

Begin by preserving the letter and recording its deadline, time zone, submission method, file-size rules, and any required form or portal. Do not use a generic deadline found online; the period in the letter applies to your case. If a document cannot be obtained in time, ask the decision-maker promptly whether an extension is possible, explain why, and do not assume the deadline changes unless you receive confirmation.

Make a response table with one row for each concern. For each row, note the fact or document questioned, the accurate explanation, the evidence that supports it, and where that evidence appears in the submission. Then build a concise cover letter that follows the same order as the concerns. Address the difficult facts directly; ignoring them can leave the concern unanswered.

Use original records where possible and explain discrepancies rather than silently changing a date or description. If documents are in another language, follow the letter’s translation instructions. Label files clearly, use the required submission channel, and save confirmation of delivery before the deadline.

A Brampton applicant may be dealing with a PFL while work, school, or family deadlines continue. Ask a trusted person to help collect records, but have the applicant verify every factual statement before sending. If the letter raises possible misrepresentation, criminality, medical inadmissibility, or a serious status consequence, contact a qualified professional immediately because the response may have long-term effects.

Officer decision notes that accompany many refusals are not the same as a PFL: notes explain an existing decision, while a PFL asks for a response before a decision is made. Keep those processes distinct.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a procedural fairness letter the same as a refusal?

No. It raises a concern and gives an opportunity to respond before a decision. Follow the specific response deadline and instructions in the letter.

### How long do I have to respond to a PFL?

There is no single deadline for every case. The date in your letter controls; request any needed extension promptly and do not assume it was granted.

### Are officer decision notes a PFL?

No. Decision notes explain an officer’s reasoning after a decision. A PFL is a notice that seeks a response before the decision is made.
