## Quick answer

Express Entry’s 2026 category-based rounds may invite eligible candidates in French, health care and social services, STEM, trades, education, transport, selected Canadian-experience occupations, or skilled military groups. A category does not replace eligibility for an Express Entry program. Brampton workers should verify the exact occupation code and experience period on IRCC’s current list.

IRCC’s 2026 Express Entry categories include French-language proficiency; health care and social services; STEM; trades; education; transport; physicians with Canadian work experience; senior managers with Canadian work experience; researchers with Canadian work experience; and skilled military recruits. The current list and category-specific eligibility are on [IRCC’s category-based selection page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/rounds-invitations/category-based-selection.html).

A category can create another way to be considered in an invitation round, but it does not replace the underlying Express Entry program requirements. A candidate must first qualify for an Express Entry-managed program and enter the pool. Meeting a category requirement does not guarantee an invitation or a particular cutoff.

## Start with the evidence, not the job title

A Brampton worker should compare actual job duties and dates with the official NOC description. Employers sometimes use internal titles that do not match the immigration classification. Prepare employment letters showing duties, hours, salary, dates, and work location, then keep supporting records such as pay statements and tax documents. For category-based selection, also check the required occupation experience period and whether the experience must be Canadian or may be gained abroad.

If you have French-language skills, check the language-test and minimum-score requirements directly. If your work falls in a health, education, trade, transport, or STEM field, verify the exact listed occupation code rather than relying on a broad industry label.

## A four-step category eligibility check

1. Confirm that you qualify for one of the Express Entry-managed programs and that your profile is active in the pool. Category selection is a way IRCC can invite a subset of eligible candidates; it is not a separate application that bypasses the pool.

2. Match your duties to the National Occupational Classification (NOC). Compare the work you actually performed with the lead statement and main duties, and identify the closest occupation code. A company’s internal title, a business-card title, or a broad sector such as “health” or “technology” is not enough by itself.

3. Check the category’s current experience requirement. Some categories require a minimum period of work in a listed occupation and specify whether it may be Canadian or foreign experience. Certain occupation-based groups have Canada-specific conditions. Read the exact category entry, eligible NOC list, and date window together.

4. Check your proof before relying on the category. Employer letters, pay evidence, tax documents, contracts, and schedules should tell a consistent story about dates, hours, and duties. Translate records where required and keep the originals.

A worker in Brampton may have a local employer, but the federal category rules are the same across Canada. If your duties changed over time, separate the periods and describe them accurately instead of stretching one job description across the whole employment history. Keep a note of the page version and date you reviewed, because category lists and round instructions can change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will IRCC invite everyone who qualifies for a 2026 category?

No. Category eligibility does not guarantee an invitation. IRCC decides whether to hold a category round, how many candidates to invite, and the ranking or cutoff that applies.

### Does a Brampton address help me qualify for a category?

No. Location in Brampton does not itself satisfy a category requirement. The relevant factors are your Express Entry program eligibility, occupation, qualifying experience, and other rules for the category.

### Can I use my job title to choose the NOC?

Treat the title as a starting clue only. Compare your actual duties with the NOC description and keep documents that support the work you claim. IRCC assesses the evidence under its rules.
