An LMIA application is an employer process under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program. It is not a way to sell a job or guarantee permanent residence. Employers must meet the rules for the applicable stream, demonstrate a genuine job and business, recruit qualified Canadians and permanent residents as required, and keep records. The [current high-wage LMIA requirements](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/foreign-workers/median-wage/high/requirements.html) were updated in September 2026, so older checklists may miss current duties.

For many high-wage positions, employers must conduct at least three recruitment activities: Job Bank plus two additional methods that reach appropriate audiences. The advertising must satisfy duration and timing rules, job postings must contain required details, and recruitment records must be retained for six years. The exact rules and exceptions vary by stream and occupation.

## Organize the Brampton employer file

Before submitting, prepare documents that show the business is operating and can meet the employment terms. Keep the job description, wage, work location, payroll structure, recruitment records, interview results, reasons candidates were not selected, and signed employment agreement. Make sure whoever controls the work and pays the employee is correctly identified as the employer.

Employers must not charge or recover recruitment fees from temporary foreign workers, directly or indirectly. Do not let a recruiter promise that an LMIA will be approved or require a worker to pay for a job offer.

As of 3 November 2026, IRCC’s Express Entry job-offer scoring rules are changing. That does not eliminate an LMIA requirement where an employer is using the Temporary Foreign Worker Program to hire a worker. Express Entry scoring and LMIA-based work authorization are separate systems.


## Set up a defensible recruitment record

Start by identifying the work location, occupation, wage stream, and whether any occupation-specific or regional rule applies. The median-wage threshold used to distinguish streams depends on the work location and current ESDC figures, so do not copy an old threshold from another city or year. Confirm the correct wage for the actual Brampton worksite and compare the offered wage with the current prevailing wage rules.

For a typical high-wage application, plan the recruitment campaign before posting. ESDC’s current guidance calls for Job Bank plus two additional methods that reach appropriate audiences, with specific timing, content, and retention rules. Keep dated copies of every advertisement, the platforms used, applicant resumes, interview notes, screening criteria, and reasons qualified Canadians or permanent residents were not selected. The current requirements specify how long advertisements must run and that employers retain recruitment records for six years; check whether an exception applies to your case.

Prepare the business file as well: proof the business is operating, payroll and financial information requested by the application, a detailed job description, wage and hours, worksite, reporting structure, and employment agreement. The role should reflect real business needs, not be written solely to match a worker’s immigration history.

Employers must not recover recruitment fees from the foreign worker, directly or indirectly. Do not let a recruiter collect money for a job offer or promise that an LMIA will be approved. If using a third party, document the service relationship and check who is responsible for each filing.

Keep the LMIA decision separate from work authorization and permanent residence. An approved LMIA can support a work-permit application in some cases, but IRCC makes that separate decision. Express Entry scoring and Ontario nomination rules are also separate processes.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many recruitment activities are required for a high-wage LMIA?

For many high-wage applications, ESDC requires Job Bank and two additional recruitment activities. Requirements can vary, so use the current rules for the specific stream and occupation.

### Can an employer charge a worker for LMIA recruitment costs?

Employers must not recover recruitment fees from temporary foreign workers, directly or indirectly. Review ESDC’s current employer obligations and ensure recruiters follow them.

### Does an approved LMIA guarantee the worker a permit or PR?

No. The LMIA is an employer-side decision. The worker may need a separate work permit, and permanent residence requires a distinct application and decision.
