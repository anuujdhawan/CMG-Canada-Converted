## Quick answer

Many post-secondary students who change to a different designated learning institution must apply to extend their study permit and follow IRCC’s school-change instructions before switching. A new acceptance letter alone may not be enough. Check whether an exception applies, whether a new PAL/TAL is needed, and whether you may study at the new DLI while an application is pending.

Changing colleges or universities can affect your study-permit conditions and future work-permit eligibility. Since 22 January 2025, many post-secondary students changing designated learning institutions (DLIs) must apply to extend their study permit and include a new letter of acceptance and a new PAL/TAL if required. IRCC’s [changing schools instructions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/change-schools.html) explain the process and limited situations where a student may begin at the new school while an extension is in progress.

If you are studying in or near Brampton, do not rely only on an advisor at either school. First confirm that the receiving institution is on IRCC’s DLI list and that the program is eligible for your plans, including any post-graduation work permit questions.

## Before you withdraw or transfer

1. Compare your current permit conditions with the proposed change in school, program, and level.
2. Ask the new DLI for a valid letter of acceptance and confirm whether a PAL/TAL is needed.
3. Check whether you need to apply for a new or extended study permit before changing institutions.
4. Keep proof of enrollment and continue meeting your current permit conditions.
5. Save copies of the application, school correspondence, and any IRCC confirmation.

Some eligible students can start at a new DLI while their application is pending, but this is not a general rule. IRCC lists specific qualifying circumstances, including certain situations where the former school closes, suspends a program, or loses its designated status. Read the full conditions before making a move.

Changing schools without following the required process can create problems with status and future applications. If you are uncertain, pause the transfer decision long enough to verify the rule rather than assuming that a new acceptance letter alone is enough.


## Use this decision sequence before changing schools

First, identify what is changing. Moving to a different campus of the same DLI, changing programs at the same school, and transferring to a new DLI can have different consequences. Check the school’s DLI number and the conditions on your permit rather than relying on a college adviser’s general description.

If you are moving to another post-secondary DLI, follow IRCC’s current process for a new study permit or extension. Gather a valid letter of acceptance from the receiving institution and determine whether a PAL/TAL is required for your application category. IRCC describes limited situations in which an eligible student may begin at the new school while an extension is being processed. Do not assume this applies to every transfer: confirm the facts and conditions listed on the official page.

Before withdrawing, ask both schools for written dates: when your current enrollment ends, when the new program begins, and whether the receiving school can issue the required documents. Save transcripts, tuition records, correspondence, and proof of any application submission. Maintain your current status and comply with the existing permit conditions while the change is underway.

A transfer can affect more than attendance. It may influence future PGWP eligibility, the program length used for a work-permit assessment, or your ability to work while studying. Confirm the receiving program’s DLI and PGWP status independently with IRCC; marketing statements are not a guarantee.

If the new school start date is close, ask IRCC’s official support channel or an authorized representative to assess whether you can begin classes before a decision. Avoid making an irreversible withdrawal based only on an assumption.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I change schools with only a new letter of acceptance?

Often, a post-secondary student changing DLIs must follow IRCC’s permit-change process as well. The letter is important, but it may not be the only requirement; check the current instructions and any applicable exception.

### Can I start at the new school while my permit application is pending?

Only in circumstances set out by IRCC. Read the current conditions carefully and confirm that your situation fits before attending or working under the new arrangement.

### Will transferring schools affect my PGWP?

It can. PGWP eligibility depends on the DLI, program, study history, and other criteria. Check the new program’s eligibility before transferring and keep records from both institutions.
