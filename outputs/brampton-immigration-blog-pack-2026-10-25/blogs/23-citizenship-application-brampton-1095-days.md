## Quick answer

Most adult citizenship applicants need 1,095 days of physical presence in Canada during the five years before applying. Some pre-PR time in Canada may count as half-days, up to a maximum credit of 365 days. Applicants must also meet other requirements, which can include tax filing, language proof, and a citizenship test.

Most adult applicants must have been physically present in Canada for at least 1,095 days during the five years before applying for citizenship. Some time in Canada as a temporary resident or protected person may count as half-days, up to a maximum of 365 days. Other requirements also apply, including tax-filing obligations and, for many applicants aged 18 to 54, proof of language ability. Review the official [citizenship eligibility requirements](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/adult-minor/who.html).

IRCC encourages applicants to use its online physical-presence calculator and to apply with more than the minimum where possible, so a date-counting mistake does not put the application below the threshold. The calculator’s result does not replace your responsibility to give complete and accurate travel dates.

## Make a Brampton citizenship file

Prepare a five-year calendar of travel outside Canada, addresses, work or study history, and any time before permanent residence that might count. Compare passport stamps, tickets, travel emails, and other records. Keep the calculation printout and correct any missing dates before signing the application.

Check the current forms and accepted language evidence. Confirm whether your tax-filing obligations were met for the required years. Do not assume that meeting the day count alone completes the eligibility test.

If you moved to Brampton recently, prior time in another Canadian city still counts as Canadian physical presence. The question is where you were physically present, not which municipality you lived in.

A Canadian citizenship application can have serious consequences if travel dates or history are incomplete. Ask for a file review if your record includes long or frequent trips, previous inadmissibility concerns, or uncertain temporary status.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need for Canadian citizenship?

Most adult applicants need 1,095 days of physical presence in the five years before applying. Other eligibility requirements also apply.

### Can time in Canada before PR count?

Some eligible time as a temporary resident or protected person may count as half-days, up to a maximum credit of 365 days. Use IRCC’s current calculator and rules.

### Do I need to live in Brampton for a certain period to apply?

No. The physical-presence calculation counts days in Canada, not days in a specific city. Your broader eligibility and address history still need to be reported accurately.
