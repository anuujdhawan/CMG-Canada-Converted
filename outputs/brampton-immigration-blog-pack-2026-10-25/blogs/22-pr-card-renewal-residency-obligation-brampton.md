## Quick answer

A permanent resident generally must meet a 730-day residency obligation in each five-year period. If you have been a PR for five years or more, IRCC assesses the five years before the application; if you became a PR more recently, you must be able to meet 730 days by your fifth anniversary. Some time abroad counts only in limited circumstances.

Permanent residents generally need to meet the residency obligation to renew a PR card. ## Calculate the five-year period before booking travel

Begin with your permanent-resident landing date and make a complete travel timeline. For a person who has been a PR for at least five years, count days in the five years immediately before the PR card application. For someone who became a PR less than five years ago, look forward to the fifth anniversary and check whether the current days in Canada plus planned days can reach 730. Use IRCC’s current guide and calculator instructions for the exact dates.

Some time outside Canada may count in specific situations, such as accompanying a Canadian-citizen spouse or working abroad full-time for a qualifying Canadian business or public service. These are fact-specific exceptions with evidence requirements. Do not count every trip abroad as eligible time simply because you were employed or travelling with family.

Keep proof of each entry and exit, including passports, tickets, travel itineraries, and any official travel history available to you. If relying on an exception, collect documents that show the relationship, employer, assignment, and qualifying status. Keep a spreadsheet, but reconcile it to the source records before signing the application.

A PR card is proof of status and a travel document; its expiry does not by itself end permanent-resident status. At the same time, an expired card can make commercial travel to Canada difficult. A residency-obligation problem may have more serious consequences than a routine renewal. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I lose PR status when my PR card expires?

No. An expired card does not by itself end permanent-resident status. The residency obligation is assessed under separate rules, and the card is used as proof and for travel.

### How many days must I be in Canada to renew a PR card?

Generally, 730 days in the relevant five-year period. The calculation differs depending on whether you have been a permanent resident for five years or less.

### Can days abroad count toward the 730-day requirement?

Some time abroad may count in limited circumstances, such as certain accompanying-spouse or Canadian-business employment situations. Check IRCC’s rules and retain evidence for the specific exception.
