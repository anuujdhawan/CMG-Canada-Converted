## Quick answer

For a 2026 Canadian study-permit application, first determine whether you need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL/TAL). IRCC’s exemptions depend on the student’s category, institution, program, and application type; otherwise, a valid attestation letter may be required. A Brampton home address alone does not decide which province issues it.

Canada’s 2026 study-permit cap and attestation-letter rules make it important to check the exact applicant category before submitting an application. IRCC expects to issue up to 408,000 study permits in 2026, including new arrivals and extensions. For 2026, master’s and doctoral students enrolled at public designated learning institutions (DLIs), primary and secondary students, certain priority groups, and some same-school, same-level extension applicants are exempt from the PAL/TAL requirement. Other applicants may need a valid provincial or territorial attestation letter. See IRCC’s [2026 allocation notice](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/news/notices/2026-provincial-territorial-allocations-under-international-student-cap.html) and [PAL/TAL instructions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/provincial-attestation-letter.html).

A Brampton address does not determine the province that issues the attestation letter. The intended study location and the applicant’s category matter. Check the DLI, the program, the application date, and whether an exception applies before paying deposits or relying on an exemption.

## A practical PAL/TAL and study-permit preflight

Use the checklist in this order so you do not rely on an exemption that does not fit your case:

1. Confirm your exact student category and the date you plan to apply. IRCC’s 2026 rules include exemptions for some groups, including qualifying master’s and doctoral students at public DLIs, primary and secondary students, and certain extensions or priority categories. The details and evidence requirements matter.
2. Confirm the DLI, campus, program, level of study, and intended province. A letter from a school is not a PAL/TAL unless it meets the provincial or territorial instructions.
3. If you are not exempt, ask the relevant province or territory how to obtain its attestation letter and whether its allocation process is open. Do not assume that paying a tuition deposit guarantees a letter or a study permit.
4. Prepare the rest of the application: letter of acceptance, proof of funds, identity and family documents, academic records, and a study plan that explains why the program fits your background. Follow the instructions for the country where you apply.

The 2026 cap figure is a national target, not a promise that a particular application will be approved. A PAL/TAL addresses one part of the application; an officer still assesses the full study-permit criteria. If refused, use the refusal information and any officer decision notes to identify the concern before deciding whether to reapply.

Before paying non-refundable costs, confirm both the school’s current DLI status and IRCC’s live checklist. Keep copies of the letter and the dated instructions you relied on.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every international student need a PAL or TAL in 2026?

No. IRCC lists exemptions for specific categories. If no exemption applies, the applicant generally needs the required provincial or territorial attestation letter with the study-permit application.

### Does a Brampton address mean Ontario must issue my PAL?

Your home address alone does not answer the question. Check the intended study location, DLI, program, and current provincial and IRCC instructions for your situation.

### Does a PAL guarantee that my study permit will be approved?

No. It is one required document for applicants who need it. IRCC still assesses the complete application, including the study plan, finances, admissibility, and other requirements.
