## Evidence to consider

Also prepare evidence that the relationship is genuine, not solely for immigration status. ## Turn the 12-month period into a clear evidence timeline

Make a month-by-month address chart for the full cohabitation period. Record when you began living together, any temporary absences, address changes, and the documents available for each period. A short temporary absence does not necessarily end cohabitation, but explain its dates and reason and check IRCC’s definition.

Use a range of genuine records where available: a joint lease or rental agreement, shared utilities, government or financial mail showing the same address, insurance or benefit records, and documents about shared household responsibilities. If only one partner is named on a lease or bill, do not alter it. Add other records and a short explanation of the living arrangement. The goal is to show how the evidence fits together, not to produce the largest possible pile of paper.

The application also asks about the relationship itself. ### Do both partners need to be on the same lease?

A joint lease can help, but IRCC lists different examples of evidence. If only one partner is named, provide other credible records and explain the arrangement accurately.

### Can we use screenshots as proof of a common-law relationship?
