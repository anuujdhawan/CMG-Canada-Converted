## Quick answer

Eligible international students may work up to 24 hours per week off campus while classes are in session and may work unlimited hours during a scheduled break if they meet IRCC’s conditions. Count hours across all employers. Required co-op placements follow separate rules; since 1 April 2026, eligible post-secondary students do not need a separate co-op work permit.

International students in Brampton should keep two sets of work rules separate: off-campus work during regular school terms, and placements that are part of an eligible study program. IRCC currently allows eligible students to work up to 24 hours per week off campus while classes are in session. During a scheduled school break, they may work unlimited off-campus hours if they meet the conditions. The student is responsible for tracking hours and meeting every condition on the study permit. See IRCC’s [off-campus work rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/work-off-campus.html).

A separate change took effect on 1 April 2026: eligible post-secondary students no longer need a separate co-op work permit for required placements such as internships. This streamlines the permit process, but it does not raise the 24-hour off-campus limit or remove the other study-permit conditions. Review the [co-op work-permit update](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/news/notices/simplifying-co-op-work-permit-requirement-post-secondary-international-students.html) and the eligibility criteria before a placement begins.

## Build a simple compliance record

Keep a weekly work log that records employer, work dates, and hours. Save schedules, pay statements, and school calendars. Check whether the hours are off-campus work, a required placement, or work during a scheduled break; the category matters.

If you have more than one job, count hours across all jobs. Do not assume that multiple employers create separate weekly limits. If your school program has no scheduled breaks, do not create a break by treating a statutory holiday as one.

Students may be tempted to work more when rent and tuition are high. In Brampton, make a budget and work schedule before accepting shifts that could conflict with the permit conditions. If your status, enrollment, or program changes, verify the rule before continuing to work.


## How to track hours without guessing

Use a weekly log that includes each employer, the dates and hours worked, whether classes were in session, and whether any work was an eligible required placement. Keep schedules and pay records so your log can be checked later. If you have two jobs, combine the hours for off-campus work; the weekly limit applies to you, not separately to each employer.

Confirm that your study permit contains the work authorization and that you meet the conditions for off-campus work. Keep your enrollment active and follow any requirements about full-time study, with the exceptions IRCC recognizes. A scheduled break must be part of your school’s academic calendar; a single holiday or a self-created gap is not automatically a school break.

Required co-op, internship, or practicum work needs to be an integral part of the program and meet the current IRCC conditions. The 1 April 2026 change removed the separate co-op work-permit requirement for eligible post-secondary students, but it did not turn every job into a placement or remove study-permit conditions. Keep the school’s confirmation that the work is required and its placement dates.

If hours have already exceeded the limit, stop and get case-specific advice promptly. Do not alter timesheets or ask an employer to misstate the schedule. In Brampton, where students may juggle several shifts and commuting between campus and work, a realistic calendar can help protect both academic progress and immigration compliance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I work more than 24 hours off campus during a regular term?

Eligible students are limited to 24 hours per week off campus while classes are in session. Add the hours from all off-campus jobs and follow the conditions in your permit.

### Can I work unlimited hours during a school break?

Eligible students may work unlimited off-campus hours during a regularly scheduled break if they meet IRCC’s conditions. Confirm the break on your school calendar and keep proof.

### Do I still need a co-op work permit in 2026?

Since 1 April 2026, eligible post-secondary students no longer need a separate co-op work permit for required placements. The placement must still meet the current program and IRCC conditions.
