## Quick answer

A Canadian host’s invitation letter explains the visitor’s purpose, stay, accommodation, and who will pay, but it does not guarantee a visa. The visitor must submit their own complete application and show they meet temporary-resident requirements. A Brampton host should make every statement accurate and consistent with the visitor’s forms and evidence.

A letter of invitation can help explain a visitor’s trip, but it does not guarantee a visitor visa. IRCC says the visa officer assesses the applicant under Canada’s immigration law. The host should write truthfully, describe the visit, and avoid promises they cannot keep. See the official [invitation-letter instructions](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/letter-invitation.html).

A Brampton host should include their contact details, job title, immigration status in Canada, and information about their family. The visitor’s section should identify the person, explain the relationship and purpose of the visit, state the intended dates and where the visitor will stay, and explain who will pay for expenses. Attach proof of the host’s status if IRCC’s instructions call for it.

## Keep the invitation consistent with the application

The invitation should match the visitor’s own forms and supporting evidence. If the host says they will pay for accommodation but the visitor’s application says they will stay elsewhere, explain the difference. A clear itinerary and realistic visit length help the officer understand the plan.

The visitor should also prepare their own evidence about finances, travel history, purpose of travel, and reasons they will leave Canada at the end of the authorized stay. An invitation from a family member in Brampton does not replace the visitor’s responsibility to demonstrate that they meet the requirements.

For a parent or grandparent applying for a Super Visa, the invitation must include additional commitments, including a financial-support promise and household-size information. Use the Super Visa checklist rather than adapting an ordinary visitor-letter template.

A well-written letter is specific and accurate, not dramatic. Avoid claiming that a visitor will remain permanently if the application is for a temporary visit, and never submit false employment or financial information.


## What to include and what to leave to the visitor

A useful letter identifies the host by full name, address, telephone number, job or occupation, and status in Canada. It identifies the visitor, their relationship to the host, the purpose and length of the visit, the intended dates, where they will stay, and who will cover travel and living expenses. Include the family information and proof of status that IRCC’s current invitation-letter instructions request.

The visitor remains responsible for the visa application. Their file should explain why they are travelling, how the trip will be funded, where they will stay, their travel history where relevant, and their circumstances outside Canada. The host’s letter does not replace these documents or override an officer’s assessment.

Make the trip plan realistic. If a family celebration lasts one week, explain why the requested stay is longer if that is the plan. If the host will pay some expenses but the visitor will pay others, describe the split consistently in both applications. Attach only documents that support the statements made; do not send sensitive records that are not requested or relevant.

If the visitor is a parent or grandparent and the family is considering a Super Visa, use the separate eligibility and insurance requirements. An ordinary invitation letter is not enough to satisfy the Super Visa undertaking, income, or medical-insurance criteria. Likewise, an invitation for a temporary visit should not promise employment or imply that the visitor may remain permanently.

Before signing, compare the letter with the visitor’s application draft and confirm names, passport details, dates, address, and payment arrangements. Keep a copy of the signed letter and supporting records in case IRCC requests clarification.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an invitation letter guarantee a Canadian visitor visa?

No. It helps explain the visit, but IRCC assesses the visitor’s full application and eligibility. The visitor must provide their own supporting evidence.

### What should a Brampton host include in an invitation letter?

Include the host’s identity and status, the visitor’s identity and relationship, purpose and dates, accommodation, expense arrangements, and other items required by IRCC’s current instructions.

### Can I use a regular invitation letter for a Super Visa?

The letter may be part of the file, but a Super Visa has additional eligibility, financial, and insurance requirements. Follow the separate IRCC checklist.

*Check IRCC’s instructions for the visitor’s country and visa type before the host signs the letter.*
