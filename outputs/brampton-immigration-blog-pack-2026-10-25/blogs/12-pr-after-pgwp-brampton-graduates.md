## Quick answer

Yes, a graduate may pursue permanent residence after a PGWP if they qualify for an available federal or provincial pathway, but the PGWP itself does not grant PR eligibility. Brampton graduates should track permit expiry, gain only qualifying work experience, meet language and occupation criteria, and check current Express Entry and Ontario rules before choosing a route.

Yes, some graduates can apply for permanent residence after a post-graduation work permit, but a PGWP is not a PR pathway by itself. Eligibility depends on the graduate’s actual work experience, language results, education, occupation, status, and the immigration program that is open and relevant at the time.

A common federal route to assess is the Canadian Experience Class. It requires qualifying skilled work in Canada and language results; IRCC does not require education for the CEC eligibility test, although education may affect ranking. Ontario’s 2026 nominee program has also changed, including the new Workforce Priority stream, which has its own employer and applicant rules. Read the current [CEC requirements](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/who-can-apply/canadian-experience-class.html) and [Ontario Workforce Priority stream page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-workforce-priority-stream).

## Plan before the work permit clock is short

Before graduation, confirm whether your program is PGWP-eligible and what evidence is needed. After graduation, track the PGWP application window and the expiry of any current status. While working, keep employer letters, pay records, duties, hours, language scores, and a NOC comparison. These records help you assess whether your experience may fit a future PR route.

Brampton graduates may change jobs or work multiple part-time roles. Record each position separately, and do not assume that every job or hour counts toward CEC. Co-op work, unauthorized work, and some other categories are treated differently under IRCC rules.

The federal [2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/immigration-system/levels/supplementary-immigration-levels-2026-2028.html) includes a temporary initiative to transition some workers to PR, but it is not a blanket pathway for every temporary worker or PGWP holder. Wait for published criteria before relying on it.

## Compare possible routes while you still have time

A useful starting point is to compare the route’s requirements with your current record, not with a friend’s successful application. For CEC, check whether your paid skilled work in Canada meets the required duration and exclusions, and identify the language test and level required for your occupation’s TEER. For another Express Entry program, there may be education, work-history, proof-of-funds, or other requirements. Category-based selection can be relevant only when your occupation or language profile matches a current category.

Ontario’s nominee program changed in 2026. The Workforce Priority stream has pathways with separate employer and worker criteria and an Expression of Interest process. Do not assume an old OINP stream is still open or that residence in Brampton creates a nomination opportunity. Check the live Ontario page and see whether your employer and job genuinely meet the current rules.

Create a status calendar with your PGWP expiry, passport expiry, language-test expiry, employer-letter request date, and any provincial or federal deadlines. A PR profile or EOI is not a work permit and does not, by itself, extend temporary status. Check the relevant IRCC instructions before you stop work, travel, or assume you may work while an application is pending.

Keep evidence from the beginning of each job: offer, duties, hours, pay, and employer information. If your role changes, document the new duties and dates. This is especially useful when a graduate takes more than one job in Peel or moves between employers.

Finally, separate eligibility from selection. A person may meet the minimum criteria but still wait for an invitation or nomination. Program criteria and invitation patterns can change, so recheck the official pages before making a costly decision such as enrolling in another program solely to improve a future application.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a PGWP automatically lead to permanent residence?

No. It is a temporary work permit. A graduate must separately qualify for a federal or provincial permanent-residence pathway and meet its current selection requirements.

### How much Canadian work experience do I need after a PGWP?

It depends on the pathway. CEC generally requires one year or 1,560 hours of qualifying skilled work in Canada within the relevant three-year period, but other routes have different requirements.

### Will an Express Entry profile extend my PGWP?

No. Creating a profile does not extend a work permit or authorize work. Check IRCC’s current status rules and apply for any needed extension or other status before expiry.
