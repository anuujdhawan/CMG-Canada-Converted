## Quick answer

For an OINP job-offer pathway, the employer may need to submit the position in Ontario’s Employer Portal before the worker registers an Expression of Interest. The employer and worker have separate eligibility rules and deadlines. A Brampton employer should confirm the current stream requirements, job details, portal sequence, and responsible contacts before starting.

Ontario’s redesigned 2026 nominee program makes the employer an early part of the process for job-offer pathways. Under the Ontario Workforce Priority stream, an employer initiates the process in the Employer Portal before an eligible worker can register an Expression of Interest. The employer and applicant then have separate responsibilities and timelines. Review the current [Ontario stream requirements](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-workforce-priority-stream) and [application process](https://www.ontario.ca/page/oinp-application-process) before starting.

## Prepare the business and job details

Before using the portal, the Brampton employer should confirm:

- the legal business identity and who is authorized to submit information
- the position’s duties, NOC/TEER classification, wage, hours, work location, and permanent or full-time terms
- whether the proposed role and employer meet the current stream rules
- who will prepare the worker’s EOI information and monitor invitations
- how the business will retain the offer, payroll, and supporting records

The job description should describe the work that will actually be performed. Avoid copying a generic template if it does not match the workplace. The employer and employee should resolve differences in duties or wage before filing.

Ontario’s OINP process is separate from a federal Labour Market Impact Assessment. A job offer used for a provincial nomination does not automatically mean an LMIA is required, and an LMIA does not guarantee an OINP invitation. Employers should identify which program they are using before advertising or making promises to a worker.

## Keep the timeline visible

Ontario’s current process sets a short window to register the worker’s EOI after a job offer is submitted, and separate employer and applicant deadlines after an invitation. Assign a named person to check portal messages and save proof of each submission. If a deadline or eligibility question is unclear, verify it on the official Ontario page before acting.

A careful employer file protects both the business and the worker from avoidable inconsistencies. A local immigration services consultation may help clarify program roles, but the official stream rules control.


## What the employer and worker should align

Treat the portal submission as the beginning of a coordinated file, not a formality. The employer should designate an authorized staff member, confirm the legal business identity, and gather the information Ontario requests about the business and position. The worker should independently check their pathway eligibility and prepare consistent personal, education, language, and work-history information.

Before submission, compare the signed offer, job description, payroll plan, and portal fields. Confirm who directs the employee’s day-to-day work, who pays them, where the work will be done, whether the hours and wage meet current rules, and whether the offer satisfies the stream’s terms. A generic occupation label can hide material differences in actual duties, so make the role description specific and truthful.

Create a shared timeline with the portal submission date, the EOI registration window, invitation expiry, employer forms, and applicant filing deadline as applicable. Ontario can set short deadlines; use the dates shown in the official account or notice rather than a calendar copied from an older guide. Save confirmations and copies of every filed version.

A provincial job-offer pathway is not automatically an LMIA application. Employers should identify whether they are pursuing OINP, the federal Temporary Foreign Worker Program, or both, then apply the rules for each process separately. Do not promise the employee an invitation or nomination: Ontario controls selection, and IRCC decides the later permanent-residence application.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who uses the OINP Employer Portal?

The employer uses the portal for the employer-side job-offer information where required by the selected pathway. The worker remains responsible for their own eligibility, EOI, and application steps under Ontario’s instructions.

### Does an OINP job offer need an LMIA?

An OINP job-offer process and a federal LMIA process are separate. Whether an LMIA is needed depends on the work authorization route and current rules; do not assume one submission replaces the other.

### What happens after the portal submission?

The worker may need to register an EOI within the period Ontario specifies. Registration is not an invitation. If invited, the employer and worker must complete their respective steps by the deadlines in the current instructions.
