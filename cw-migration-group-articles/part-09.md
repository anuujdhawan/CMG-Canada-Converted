### The Remaining Relative Visa (Subclass 115): Australia's "Last Family Member" Category

Among family visas, the subclass 115 has the narrowest gate in the program: it exists for the person who has no closer family anywhere — no parent, no sibling, no partner, no child — other than a relative in Australia willing to sponsor them. It's the visa of last resort, designed with the pessimism its category demands: the law assumes someone is trying to stretch a distant relationship into migration unless proven otherwise. If your situation is genuinely the one this visa was built for, here's what the gate actually requires.

**Who qualifies — the strict relationship list.** The sponsored person must be a *remaining relative* of the Australian sponsor: a parent, sibling, step-sibling, or step-parent (and their equivalents on the sponsor's side, including for onshore applications via the 835). Not cousins, not aunts, not friends-who-are-family — the list is short and legal.

**The two tests that decide everything:**

1. **The "no closer relative" test — abroad.** The applicant must have no parent, sibling, partner, or child *anywhere* — living or, in defined cases, deceased — other than in Australia. Where such a relative exists but cannot be located or has lost contact, the applicant must show a genuinely searchable absence: documented attempts over time, missing-persons evidence where applicable. "We don't talk" is not the test; "cannot be found" is.
2. **The "no closer relative" test — in Australia, capable of sponsoring.** Even a relative *in* Australia defeats the application if that relative could sponsor them under a better-fitting category — or if any other Australian relative *could be sponsored themselves*. The category demands that the Australian sponsor is genuinely the only door, and the family composition evidence must prove the negative comprehensively: statements, records, and documentation tracing the entire family tree.

**The other standard elements:**

- **Sponsorship** by the qualifying Australian relative, with the sponsor's eligibility assessed as usual.
- **An assurance of support** — the formal financial commitment family visas of this type carry.
- **Health and character** — full admissibility screens.
- **Genuine relationship evidence** — because a category built on "last family" invites scrutiny of whether the family connection is real and ongoing.

**Where 115 applications actually fail:**

1. **The family-tree dragnet finds someone.** A half-sibling abroad who was "forgotten," a parent's second family, a long-lost brother located during verification — the category's whole structure collapses if any closer relative surfaces anywhere. The family tree must be complete *before* lodgement, documented, and honest.
2. **"Cannot be found" asserted without proof.** Contact-attempt records, search evidence, and third-party documentation are what the test demands; assertions aren't enough.
3. **A better category existed.** A parent who could have been sponsored under the parent program, or a sibling eligible elsewhere — the "no other door" test defeats cases that overlooked an available lane.
4. **Assurance and financial shortfalls** — the supporting architecture fails even when the relationship case stands.

**The honest strategic note.** The 115 is slow — it sits in the family queue's long end — and it's unforgiving in a way few categories are: the evidentiary task is proving *absence*, which is harder than proving presence. Families considering it should:

1. **Map the complete family tree first** — every relative of the applicant, every relative of the sponsor, on paper, with documents.
2. **Test the alternatives honestly** — is there truly no parent/sibling/partner/child pathway? Would the remaining-relative framing survive scrutiny?
3. **Start the "cannot be found" documentation early** — search attempts, records, and evidence take time to accumulate.
4. **Run the long-queue math** — the wait is long enough that visitor-visa time together should be the parallel plan regardless.

**Where professional guidance is essential.** This is the family category where category selection itself is the decisive skill: an honest early assessment of whether the two "no closer relative" tests can actually be met — before years are invested in a doomed application. The Remaining Relative visa is Australia's acknowledgment that some people's only family is already here. It's a genuine, permanent answer — for the cases that survive the strictest definition in the family program. Prove the absence completely, and the category works exactly as designed.

### The Subclass 189: Australia's Purest Points Test — Permanent Residence, No Sponsor Required

Among skilled visas, the subclass 189 Skilled Independent visa holds a special status: permanent residence on points alone — no state nomination, no employer, no family sponsor. It's the most competitive door in the skilled program precisely because it's the most self-contained: your age, English, experience, and qualifications versus everyone else's. Here's how the 189 works, who realistically wins it, and how to decide whether to chase it or pivot to the nominated streams.

**The machinery, end to end:**

1. **Eligibility foundations.** Your occupation on the relevant skilled list, a positive skills assessment from the occupation's assessing authority, age under 45 at invitation, and competent English as the floor.
2. **The points test.** Age, English band, skilled experience (overseas and Australian), qualifications, and other factors convert to a score. For the 189, that score is *everything* — there's no nomination bonus to lean on.
3. **The Expression of Interest.** Your claims enter SkillSelect, ranked by score within your occupation group. Every claim must be evidence-backed, because an invitation triggers a document-check.
4. **The invitation.** The Department runs rounds; when the round reaches your score, you're invited. This is the 189's signature uncertainty: cutoffs move by occupation and year, and no one can promise the number that clears.
5. **Lodgement and grant.** Sixty days to lodge a complete application after invitation — medicals, clearances, evidence all in order — then permanent residence.

**What permanent residence actually means here:** immediate PR with no strings — live and work anywhere, any employer, any state, Medicare, and the citizenship pathway on the usual residence grounds. That freedom is exactly why the competition is fierce: everyone who *can* win the 189 wants it.

**The honest competitiveness conversation.** The 189's invitation rounds favour specific profiles at specific times: younger applicants, superior English, deep experience, in-demand occupations. Cutoffs swing — sometimes dramatically by occupation — which means:

- **Your friend's invited score is history, not prophecy.** Rounds move; only current data forecasts anything.
- **Points improvements compound.** Superior English (20 points), additional experience years, credential upgrades — each lever moved is hundreds of places gained.
- **The waiting period has a cost.** Months in the pool are months not spent building Australian experience or state ties — the strategic cost of the pure-points game.

**The strategic comparison every skilled applicant should run:**

| Factor | 189 Independent | 190 Nominated | 491 Regional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor needed | None | State nomination (+5 points) | Regional nomination (+15 points) |
| Competitive score demanded | Highest | Lower | Lower still |
| Restrictions | None | Commitment to nominating state | Regional residence, 5-year provisional → 191 PR |
| Speed to PR | Immediate | Immediate | ~5 years via 191 |

The pattern that emerges for most applicants: the 189 is the *preference*, the 190 and 491 are the *probability*. The strongest strategies pursue both simultaneously — a 189-calibre EOI that also courts state nominations — rather than betting everything on independence.

**Where 189 campaigns actually fail:**

1. **Points claimed beyond the evidence.** The inflated EOI that collapses at invitation — the most catastrophic and most preventable failure.
2. **Occupation rigidity.** Refusing to consider related ANZSCO occupations whose cutoffs are friendlier, when duties genuinely support them.
3. **The passive pool sit.** Lodging an EOI and waiting years without moving the levers or adding nomination angles.
4. **Timeline neglect.** Age points decline, skills assessments and English results expire — the pool punishes drift.

**Where professional guidance pays.** The 189 decision is a forecasting exercise: current cutoffs, occupation strategy, points levers, and the honest comparison with nominated streams. ### The Subclass 190: How State Nomination Turns a Good Points Score Into a Permanent Visa

The subclass 190 Skilled Nominated visa is the skilled program's most practical instrument: permanent residence like the 189 — but with a state or territory standing behind your application, adding five points and, more importantly, a selection pathway that doesn't depend solely on federal invitation rounds. For the majority of skilled applicants whose scores are good-but-not-189-competitive, the 190 is where plans actually succeed. Here's how nomination works and how to win one.

**The structure:**

1. **The same skilled foundations** — occupation on the list, positive skills assessment, age under 45, competent English floor, points-tested.
2. **The nomination layer.** A state or territory government selects you from SkillSelect (or a state application), assesses you against *its own* criteria, and nominates — which adds five points and makes you eligible for the 190's invitation pathway.
3. **Lodgement and grant.** Permanent residence, with one structural feature: a commitment to live in the nominating state for a defined initial period — nominally two years, with the expectation of genuine settlement.

**What the state is actually doing — the logic that explains every criterion.** When a state nominates you, it spends a limited annual allocation on a person it believes will live, work, and contribute in *its* territory. Every state criterion flows from that bet:

- **State occupation lists** — often narrower than the federal list, targeted at the state's shortages. Your occupation may be closed in one state and actively sought in another.
- **Residence and ties requirements** — some states require you to live there already, hold an in-state job offer, or show genuine commitment (family, prior study, a credible plan).
- **Points and experience floors** — some states set their own minimums above the federal ones.
- **Windows and caps** — nomination places are finite and open in rounds; timing is part of the strategy.

**The strategy that actually wins nominations:**

1. **Map your occupation across every state — not just the famous ones.** The state that wants you may not be the one you've heard of. Each jurisdiction's current list and criteria is a separate opportunity.
2. **Build genuine commitment evidence.** In-state residence, employment, study history, family — or a specific, credible settlement plan. Generic "I'm happy to live anywhere" commitments read exactly as they are.
3. **Respect the criteria as an exam.** States reject nominable-on-paper applicants for technical non-compliance constantly — a missed declaration, an outdated document, an unmatched points claim.
4. **Track the windows.** Rounds open on state calendars; being application-ready when yours opens converts opportunities that "almost ready" applicants miss.
5. **Run the 189 in parallel.** The 190 doesn't displace your independent competitiveness — a strong EOI serves both doors, and nomination offers can arrive while you wait for federal rounds.

**The commitment you're accepting — honestly weighed.** Nominating states expect genuine settlement: living, working, contributing. Applicants who treat nomination as a points coupon and relocate immediately after grant damage their own record — and the integrity of a system whose value depends on states trusting it. The honest approach: nominate where you'd genuinely be happy living, and the commitment costs nothing because it's real.

**Where 190 campaigns actually fail:**

- **Single-state fixation** — one closed list becomes a lost year.
- **Criteria technicalities** — the small non-compliances that defeat nominable applicants.
- **Commitment cases that read hollow** — nomination statements assembled in an afternoon.
- **Timing naïveté** — discovering your state's cap or closure after you were finally ready.

**The family dimension.** The 190 is permanent residence for the whole included family — partner and dependents — from day one, which is part of why it outvalues its five points so dramatically.

### The Subclass 191: The Quiet Finish Line of Australia's Regional Pathway

The subclass 191 is the skilled program's most misunderstood visa: the permanent residence step that regional provisional holders are actually riding toward. If you hold (or plan to hold) a 491 Skilled Work Regional visa — or the employer-sponsored regional 494 — the 191 is your destination: permanent residence after the required years of living and working in regional Australia. Its requirements are simpler than its reputation, and its income rule is the part everyone gets wrong. Here's how the finish line works.

**Who the 191 serves — its two streams:**

1. **The Temporary Residence Transition-like regional stream:** for holders of the 491 (or 494) who have lived, worked, and — where required — held their visa in regional Australia for the qualifying period (three years under current settings, though settings deserve verification against the current rules).
2. **The Hong Kong and BNO stream:** a separate pathway with its own conditions for that cohort.

**The requirements, plainly:**

- **Regional residence.** You must have lived only in designated regional areas while holding the provisional visa — the requirement is cumulative and audited through your address history, employment records, and travel movements. The "regional" definition is broader than most people assume (it covers most of Australia outside the major metro cores — including Perth, Adelaide, the Gold Coast, and Canberra under current definitions), which surprises applicants who imagined outback-only living.
- **Regional employment.** Work in any occupation — a deliberate design feature: your regional years don't need to be in your nominated occupation, just genuinely regional.
- **The tax records.** You provide Australian tax returns for the qualifying years as evidence of income — and here's the famous rule, stated correctly: **there is no minimum income threshold.** The earlier design included a salary floor; it was removed. What remains is the *provision* of tax records — you must have filed, not necessarily have earned a set amount. Applicants still refusing regional moves over a phantom income requirement are refusing a rule that no longer exists.
- **Compliance history.** Your years on the 491/494 must be clean — conditions followed, regional commitments honoured.

**The compliance story that decides the 191 before it's ever lodged.** The 191 isn't won in its application year; it's won across the three regional years:

1. **Live where you say you live.** Address history is checked against designated-area maps. Cheating the regional requirement — metro addresses, boundary-dodging — poisons the permanent application.
2. **Keep working, keep filing.** Employment records and tax returns across all qualifying years are the evidence spine. Gaps and unfiled years create the exact holes the assessment will probe.
3. **Honour the 491's conditions.** The provisional visa's obligations and the state's expectations — honouring them is the record the 191 examines.
4. **Travel and moves documented.** Internal relocations within regional Australia are fine (the requirement is regional, not one town); trips that raise residence questions should be explainable.

**The strategic view — why the 491→191 arc deserves more respect than it gets.** The regional pathway trades patience for accessibility: fifteen bonus points at nomination, friendlier state criteria, dedicated regional occupation access — in exchange for three-plus years of regional living before permanence. For thousands of applicants, it's not the compromise option; it's the *only* option that closes. And the 191's grant is permanent residence with no restrictions: live anywhere, work anywhere, the citizenship pathway open on the usual grounds.

**Where 191 applications actually fail:**

- **The residence audit fails.** Metro drift — real addresses in excluded areas, casual work trips treated as residence — the failure that no documentation rescues.
- **Unfiled tax years.** The record provision requirement isn't about income; it's about compliance. File every year.
- **Condition breaches during the provisional years** — each one is a permanent-stage question.
- **Assumption paralysis.** Applicants delaying regional moves over the removed income threshold — the strategy's most costly myth.
